# Module 4 — TRANSLATION
**Mythic Rehearsal**

> *Fluid, mythic storytelling — creative, archetypal.*

## Purpose

Identity shifts through story, not argument. M4 takes the dialectic from M3 and moves it out of the analytical register into the mythic one. The practitioner rewrites the origin story of their constraint — not to falsify it, but to hold it in a larger frame where their agency is visible alongside their wound.

## Framework: F3 — Archetypal Rehearsal

Narrative Psychology (Bruner) + Jungian Archetypes + CBT Behavioral Rehearsal.

The practitioner identifies which archetype has been governing their constraint pattern (the Wounded Child, the Exile, the Perfectionist, the Invisible One, the Protector) and which archetype carries the Gift expression of their Gene Key.

Then they write — or speak, or draw — the **Sovereign Narrative**: the story of the constraint told from the perspective of its Gift expression. This is not toxic positivity. It is not "everything happens for a reason." It is the practitioner stepping into the version of themselves who survived, learned, and now gets to choose.

### The Narrative Structure

1. **Origin** — What happened. The real conditions. No softening.
2. **The Brilliant Adaptation** — What the constraint did to protect. Why it was the right response then.
3. **The Cost** — What the pattern has been taking in the present that it no longer needs to take.
4. **The Reclamation** — What becomes available when the energy of the Shadow is expressed as Gift.
5. **The Mantra** — A single line that the practitioner can return to in moments of activation. Drawn from the Gift expression of their Gene Key.

## Module Output

A **Sovereign Narrative** (written, spoken, or illustrated) + a **Mantra** (one line, first person, present tense, Gift-voiced).

This becomes the identity anchor for M6's action architecture.

## Provenance

- Narrative Psychology: Jerome Bruner, *Actual Minds, Possible Worlds* (1986)
- Jungian Archetypes: Carl Jung, *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*
- CBT Behavioral Rehearsal: Aaron Beck
